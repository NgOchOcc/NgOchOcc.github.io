  • Adaptive Prompting for Continual Relation Extraction: Within-Task Variance Perspective
    Minh Le¹*, Ngoc Luu¹*, An Nguyen¹*, Thanh Thien, Trang Nguyen, Tung Nguyen, Linh Ngo, Thien Huu Nguyen
    AAAI 2025 • Oral Presentation

    Abstract: Continual learning for relation extraction faces the challenge of catastrophic forgetting when learning new tasks. This paper proposes an adaptive prompting mechanism that captures within-task variance in prompt learning. Our approach enables models to learn discriminative prompts that adapt to task-specific characteristics while maintaining knowledge from previous tasks. We demonstrate that considering variance in the prompt space significantly improves continual relation extraction performance. Experimental results on multiple benchmarks show our method achieves state-of-the-art performance with minimal forgetting on previously learned relations.

  • Optimizing Answer Generator in Vietnamese Legal Question Answering Systems
    Ngoc Luu¹*, Huong Le, Thanh Nguyen, Tuan Dao, Sang Dinh
    TALLIP 2024 • Published

    Abstract: Question answering systems for specialized domains like legal text require careful optimization of answer generation components. This paper presents a comprehensive study on optimizing answer generation in Vietnamese legal QA systems. We propose a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach combined with fine-tuned language models specifically adapted for the legal domain. Our system achieves significant improvements in answer relevance and accuracy by: (1) using domain-specific retrievers trained on legal corpora, (2) fine-tuning transformer models on legal texts, and (3) implementing post-processing mechanisms for legal terminology normalization. We evaluate our approach on a newly constructed Vietnamese legal QA dataset and achieve state-of-the-art results, demonstrating the effectiveness of domain-optimized generation strategies.
    Published: April 2024 | Journal: ACM Transactions on Asian and Low-Resource Language Information Processing (TALLIP)

  • Beyond the Frontier: Stochastic Backtracking for Efficient Test-Time Scaling
    Dao Tran, Duc Anh Le, Tien Ngoc Luu, Quan Pham, Tung Pham, Hung H Bui
    NeurIPS 2026 • Submitted

    Abstract: Test-time scaling has emerged as a powerful approach to enhance model reasoning capabilities by allocating more computation at inference time. However, existing test-time scaling methods often require careful tuning of compute budgets and can suffer from diminishing returns. This paper proposes stochastic backtracking, a novel test-time scaling strategy that adaptively explores the solution space using stochastic search with backtracking mechanisms. Our approach iteratively refines predictions by backing off from unpromising reasoning trajectories and exploring alternative paths. This enables efficient scaling with minimal wasted computation while maintaining improved performance. We demonstrate significant improvements on challenging reasoning benchmarks, showing up to 15% improvement in accuracy with modest computational overhead compared to baseline test-time approaches.
    Submitted: September 2025 | Status: Under Review at NeurIPS 2026

  • Mixture-of-Control: State-Aware Fine-Tuning for Transformer-based Models
    Duc Anh Nguyen¹*, Ngoc Luu¹*, Tung Pham, Toan Tran
    NeurIPS 2026 • Submitted

    Abstract: Fine-tuning pre-trained transformer models is the standard approach for adapting to downstream tasks, but existing methods often update all parameters uniformly without considering task-specific requirements. We propose Mixture-of-Control (MoC), a state-aware fine-tuning framework that learns adaptive control mechanisms for different layers and attention heads based on task characteristics. Our approach decomposes the fine-tuning process into a mixture of control modules that dynamically adjust learning rates and gradient flows based on the input state. This enables more efficient adaptation with fewer parameters while improving downstream task performance. We demonstrate significant improvements across multiple benchmarks including NLP and vision tasks, showing 2-5% improvement with 30% fewer trainable parameters compared to standard fine-tuning.
    Submitted: September 2025 | Status: Under Review at NeurIPS 2026

Research Overview

My research focuses on developing efficient and practical AI systems, with particular emphasis on:

  • Test-Time Scaling Strategies: Developing novel approaches to improve model performance at inference time through stochastic methods and adaptive techniques for efficient reasoning in language models. Focus on reducing computational cost while maintaining or improving performance.

  • Continual Learning: Creating adaptive mechanisms that enable models to learn from new tasks while maintaining knowledge from previous tasks, with applications to relation extraction in NLP. Investigating how to capture task-specific characteristics through prompt learning.

  • Fine-tuning Mechanisms: Exploring state-aware and control-based approaches to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning transformer-based models. Investigating how different model components require different learning strategies.

  • Domain-Specific NLP: Advancing natural language processing systems for specialized domains, particularly Vietnamese language processing. Recent work on legal domain QA and relation extraction tasks.

  • Model Optimization: Designing efficient inference techniques including quantization, knowledge distillation, and architectural improvements for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

Work in Progress & Future Directions

Currently, I am working on:

  • Stochastic Backtracking for Efficient Inference: Exploring novel test-time scaling strategies that leverage stochastic search and backtracking to enable efficient inference while maintaining improved reasoning capabilities. This work aims to reduce computational costs of advanced reasoning in large language models without sacrificing performance.

  • Mixture-of-Control Mechanisms: Developing fine-tuning frameworks that use state-aware control mechanisms to adaptively adjust model behavior during training. This approach aims to improve both training efficiency and downstream task performance across different domains.

  • Continual Learning for NLP: Extending continual learning techniques to handle more complex NLP tasks beyond relation extraction, investigating how to maintain multi-task knowledge while learning new tasks efficiently.
